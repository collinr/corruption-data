## LAYER-BY-LAYER SUMMARY

### **LAYER 1: PHILOSOPHY**

**Epistemology (How do you know?):**
- **Critical Realism:** Corruption is objectively real (cases documented); but data interpretation is socially mediated

**Ontology (What exists?):**
- **Objective:** Corruption cases, audit findings, court records
- **Socially-constructed:** Source definitions, coverage decisions, institutional biases

**Axiology (What values guide you?):**
- Transparency | Accountability | Democratic governance | Citizen oversight

**Philosophical statement for proposal:**
"This study adopts a critical realist epistemology with pragmatist values, recognising corruption as objective phenomena while acknowledging that data are socially-interpreted. Research aims to consolidate fragmented corruption data sources to support evidence-based policy."

---

### **LAYER 2: APPROACH**

**Abductive Reasoning (Primary):**
- **Observe:** South Africa has fragmented corruption data (puzzle)
- **Propose:** Systematic mapping will reveal the landscape structure
- **Test:** Implement PRISMA screening, quality appraisal, triangulation
- **Refine:** Findings reveal convergences, gaps, and insights

**Why Abductive?**
- Neither purely deductive (no hypothesis to test) nor inductive (not just exploring with no framework)
- Starts with observation of anomaly, proposes explanation, tests empirically

**Approach statement for proposal:**
"The study employs abductive reasoning, observing data fragmentation and proposing that systematic mapping reveals landscape structure. PRISMA methodology (Identification → Screening → Appraisal → Extraction → Synthesis) operationalizes this abductive logic."

---

### **LAYER 3: DESIGN**

**Descriptive (Primary):**
- RQ1: "What sources exist?" ✓ Descriptive
- RQ2: "How do they differ?" ✓ Descriptive
- RQ3: "What do data reveal?" ✓ Descriptive

**Exploratory (Secondary):**
- Triangulation may reveal unexpected patterns
- Gap analysis may reveal unexpected absences

**Why NOT Explanatory?**
- You are NOT testing causal claims ("X causes Y")
- You are NOT explaining WHY fragmentation exists
- You ARE characterizing WHAT exists

**Design statement for proposal:**
"This is a descriptive study characterising the corruption data landscape. Exploratory sub-elements emerge when triangulation reveals unexpected gaps or convergences worthy of investigation."

---

### **LAYER 4: METHOD(S)**

**Mixed Methods with Qualitative Emphasis (80/20 split):**

| Component | Qualitative (80%) | Quantitative (20%) |
|---|---|---|
| **Source characterization** | Describe scope, limitations, biases | Count sources by type/category |
| **Quality assessment** | Narrative assessment of credibility | 1–5 Likert scores × 7 dimensions |
| **Corruption cases** | Analyze narratives, context, details | Count cases; measure Rand values |
| **Triangulation** | Compare case narratives across sources | Convergence metric (# sources reporting) |
| **Gap analysis** | Describe missing sectors qualitatively | Frequency tables by sector |

**Why Mixed?**
- Qualitative = rich context, nuance, interpretation
- Quantitative = systematic comparison, structure, metrics
- Together = both depth and structure

**Methods statement for proposal:**
"This study employs mixed methods combining qualitative document analysis (Bowen, 2009) with quantitative metrics (scoring, frequency counts, convergence analysis). Qualitatively, we characterise sources and assess credibility; quantitatively, we provide structured comparison and systematic ranking."

---

### **LAYER 5: TECHNIQUES**

**Data Collection:**
- **PRISMA 5-phase systematic source mapping**
  - Phase 1: Systematic identification (search across defined repositories)
  - Phase 2: Two-stage screening (title/abstract, then full-text)
  - Phase 3: Quality appraisal (7-dimension scoring)
  - Phase 4: Data extraction (standardized form)
  - Phase 5: Synthesis & triangulation

**Data Analysis:**
- **Document Analysis** (Bowen, 2009): Interpret source content, assess credibility
- **Quality Assessment:** Score each source 1–5 across 7 dimensions (credibility, completeness, consistency, timeliness, accessibility, granularity, bias risk)
- **Content Analysis:** Code sources by characteristics (scope, definitions, accessibility barriers)
- **Triangulation:** Map which cases reported by multiple sources; count convergence
- **Gap Analysis:** Tabulate data coverage by sector; identify sparseness

**Tools:**
- Excel or Google Sheets (catalogue & quality scoring)
- Zotero or Mendeley (document storage)
- Atlas.ti or Nvivo (optional; for detailed coding)
- Visualization software (heatmaps, Venn diagrams)

**Techniques statement for proposal:**
"Data collection follows PRISMA-ScR (Tricco et al., 2018) systematic source identification. Data analysis combines Bowen's document analysis principles (Bowen, 2009) with quality assessment (7-dimension scoring), content analysis, and triangulation. All procedures are documented for transparency and reproducibility."

## COMMON QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

### **Q: Why critical realism rather than pure positivism or constructivism?**
A: Positivism assumes one objective reality, fully measurable. Constructivism assumes reality is entirely socially constructed. Critical realism acknowledges objective phenomena (corruption cases) while recognizing interpretation is socially mediated (source definitions, coverage biases). This fits your study perfectly.

### **Q: Why abductive rather than deductive?**
A: You don't have a hypothesis to test (which would be deductive). You observe a puzzle (fragmentation) and propose an explanation (systematic mapping reveals structure). This is abductive: anomaly → explanation → empirical test.

### **Q: Why descriptive rather than explanatory?**
A: You're not testing causal claims ("Why do sources differ?"). You're characterizing what exists and how it differs. This is descriptive. Later research could be explanatory.

### **Q: Why mixed methods?**
A: Qualitative methods provide rich context; quantitative provide structure. Document analysis (qualitative) characterizes sources; quality scoring (quantitative) ranks them. Together, they offer both depth and systematic comparison.

### **Q: Can the onion change as I research?**
A: Yes. Your philosophical position may be refined as you work. Document any changes in a reflexivity log and explain them in your final thesis. This shows methodological integrity, not weakness.

## FULL METHODOLOGICAL STATEMENT (Copy-Paste Template)

```
Research Onion Framework

This study's research design is structured using the research onion framework 
(Saunders, Lewis, & Thornhill, 2019), which organises methodological choices 
as five nested layers.

Philosophy: This study adopts a critical realist epistemology with pragmatist 
values. We recognise that corruption is an objective phenomenon documented in 
institutional records while acknowledging that interpretation is socially 
mediated by institutional definitions, political priorities, and media biases. 
Our goal is practical: consolidate data sources to support evidence-based policy.

Approach: The study employs abductive reasoning. We observe data fragmentation 
(the puzzle), propose that systematic mapping will reveal landscape structure 
(the explanation), and test this through PRISMA-guided source identification, 
quality appraisal, and triangulation (empirical testing).

Design: This is a descriptive study characterising the corruption data landscape. 
Primary questions ask "What sources exist?" and "How do they differ?"—both 
descriptive inquiries. The study incorporates exploratory elements where 
triangulation may reveal unexpected patterns.

Methods: The study employs mixed methods with qualitative emphasis (80% qualitative, 
20% quantitative). Qualitatively, we conduct systematic document analysis to 
characterise source scope and credibility. Quantitatively, we apply quality scoring, 
frequency analysis, and convergence metrics.

Techniques: Data collection follows PRISMA-ScR systematic source mapping in five 
phases. Data analysis combines Bowen's document analysis principles with quality 
assessment, content analysis, triangulation, and gap identification.
```

The research onion is not a rigid template—it's a framework for **thinking systematically** 
about your methodological choices. Each layer should follow logically from the layer 
above it. Your critical realist philosophy → enables abductive reasoning → justifies 
descriptive design → requires mixed methods → operationalizes through systematic 
techniques. This coherence strengthens your methodology.
