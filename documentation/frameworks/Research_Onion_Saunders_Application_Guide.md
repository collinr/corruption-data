## 2. What Is the Research Onion?

### **Conceptual Overview**

The Research Onion is a framework that visualises research methodology as **five concentric layers**, each representing a critical design decision. The metaphor of an "onion" suggests:

- **Nested structure:** Each inner layer is informed by outer layers
- **Sequential peeling:** You move from philosophical foundations inward to concrete techniques
- **Interconnectedness:** Decisions at one layer constrain or enable choices at the next layer

### **The Five Layers (Outermost to Innermost)**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Layer 1: RESEARCH PHILOSOPHY                    │
│ (Epistemology, Ontology, Axiology)             │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Layer 2: RESEARCH APPROACH                      │
│ (Deductive, Inductive, Abductive)              │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Layer 3: RESEARCH DESIGN                        │
│ (Exploratory, Descriptive, Explanatory)        │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Layer 4: RESEARCH METHOD(S)                     │
│ (Qualitative, Quantitative, Mixed)             │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Layer 5: DATA COLLECTION & ANALYSIS             │
│ (Techniques and procedures)                      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### **LAYER 2: RESEARCH APPROACH**

How will you reason from your philosophical position to your research question?

#### **2.1 Your Research Approach: ABDUCTIVE (with Inductive elements)**

**Definition:**
- **Deductive:** Theory → Hypothesis → Test with data → Confirm/refute
- **Inductive:** Observe data → Identify patterns → Build theory
- **Abductive:** Observe puzzles/contradictions → Propose plausible explanations → Test against data → Refine explanations

**Why Abductive for your study:**

Your research begins with an **observed puzzle:**
- *Puzzle:* South Africa has numerous corruption investigations (AGSA, SIU, Public Protector, etc.) but no unified, accessible corruption data landscape
- *Question:* What corruption data sources exist? How do they differ in quality and scope? What does aggregating them reveal?

**Your reasoning process is ABDUCTIVE:**

1. **Observation:** You encounter fragmentation in anti-corruption data (Zondo Commission reports, AGSA audits, media investigations exist separately)
2. **Abduction:** You propose an explanatory framework—"What if we *systematically mapped* all sources, evaluated their quality, and triangulated findings? What would that reveal about SA's corruption data landscape?"
3. **Testing:** You implement PRISMA source identification, quality appraisal (your 7-dimension matrix), and data extraction
4. **Refinement:** Findings reveal where data converges (credible), where gaps exist (unexplored sectors), and where fragmentation creates inefficiency

**Abductive statement for proposal:**

"This study employs an abductive research approach, beginning with the observed puzzle of corruption data fragmentation in South Africa. Rather than testing a pre-specified hypothesis (deductive) or purely exploring patterns (inductive), we systematically map sources and their qualities, proposing explanations for convergences and gaps, and iteratively refining our understanding of the corruption data landscape through evidence-based source evaluation."

#### **2.2 Inductive Elements Within Abduction**

You also use **induction** within your larger abductive design:

- **Inductive phase:** As you extract data from sources and conduct triangulation (Phase 5 in PRISMA), you may identify unexpected patterns—e.g., "corruption cases in water services are severely under-reported" or "whistleblower reports reveal corruption in sectors where AGSA audits find little"
- **Emerging insights:** These patterns are inductive discoveries that refine your understanding

**Multi-method reasoning:**

```
ABDUCTION (overall framing):
"What does a systematic mapping of corruption data sources reveal?"

    ↓

INDUCTION (within triangulation analysis):
"What patterns emerge when we compare cases across sources?"

    ↓

DEDUCTION (for validation):
"If this pattern is real, we should see it reflected in other sources too"
(test for convergence)
```

### **LAYER 3: RESEARCH DESIGN**

What type of study are you conducting?

#### **3.1 Your Research Design: DESCRIPTIVE (with Exploratory elements)**

**Definition:**
- **Exploratory:** Clarify understanding of unclear/emerging phenomena; "What is happening?"
- **Descriptive:** Describe characteristics, patterns, frequencies; "What does it look like?"
- **Explanatory:** Explain causal relationships; "Why is this happening?"

**Your primary design: DESCRIPTIVE**

Your study's core research questions are explicitly descriptive:

1. **RQ1:** "What publicly available data sources exist on corruption and governance in South Africa?" → **Descriptive** (inventory)
2. **RQ2:** "How do data sources differ in structure, quality, and scope?" → **Descriptive** (characterization)
3. **RQ3:** "What does the data tell us about corruption and governance in South Africa?" → **Descriptive** (synthesis)

**But with Exploratory sub-components:**

- You will *explore* data gaps and what they signify
- You will *explore* convergence across sources (triangulation reveals patterns not obvious in single sources)
- You may discover *unexpected* sector-specific insights

**Descriptive design statement for proposal:**

"This is a descriptive study employing systematic mapping methodology. The primary objective is to describe the landscape of publicly available corruption and governance data sources in South Africa—cataloguing sources, characterizing their structural and quality features, evaluating their credibility and accessibility, and synthesizing findings to present a comprehensive inventory. While primarily descriptive, the study incorporates exploratory elements in data triangulation, where unexpected patterns may reveal insights about corruption data gaps, institutional transparency, and sectoral variation."

#### **3.2 Descriptive vs. Explanatory: Why Not Explanatory?**

**You are NOT testing causal explanations** such as:
- "Digital maturity *causes* better corruption data availability" (causal)
- "Institutional capacity *determines* audit data quality" (causal)

If you wanted to test these, you'd need:
- Control groups or comparative institutional analysis
- Statistical controls for confounders
- Hypothesis-testing design

**Your study is descriptive because you are:**
- Mapping the terrain
- Characterizing what exists
- Evaluating quality
- Identifying gaps

A later study could be explanatory: "Why are certain sectors under-reported?"

### **LAYER 4: RESEARCH METHOD(S)**

Will you use qualitative, quantitative, or mixed methods?

#### **4.1 Your Research Method: MIXED METHODS (Predominantly Qualitative)**

**Definition:**
- **Quantitative:** Numerical data; statistical analysis; "How much? How many? Is there a significant relationship?"
- **Qualitative:** Textual, narrative data; thematic analysis; "What is the character, quality, or nature of this phenomenon?"
- **Mixed methods:** Both, integrated for complementarity or triangulation

**Your mixed-method design:**

| Aspect | Your Approach |
|---|---|
| **Qualitative (80%)** | Document analysis (Bowen framework); content analysis of source scope & data types; thematic coding of corruption narratives; quality assessment narratives |
| **Quantitative (20%)** | Frequency counts (# sources by category, # corruption cases by type); quality scores (1–5 Likert); composite metrics (average credibility score); convergence counts (# sources reporting same case) |
| **Integration** | Quantitative scores inform qualitative narrative; qualitative assessment explains why a source scored low; triangulation matrix combines both |

**Why Mixed Methods?**

| Component | Qualitative Justification | Quantitative Justification |
|---|---|---|
| **Source mapping** | Document each source's scope & content (narrative) | Count sources by category & type (frequency) |
| **Quality assessment** | Explain credibility criteria & limitations (narrative) | Score each source 1–5 across 7 dimensions (metric) |
| **Triangulation** | Compare case narratives across sources (content analysis) | Count how many sources report each case (convergence metric) |
| **Gap analysis** | Describe missing sectors qualitatively (narrative) | Quantify data sparseness by sector (frequency) |

**Mixed-method statement for proposal:**

"This study employs a mixed-methods approach, combining qualitative document analysis with quantitative metrics. Qualitatively, we conduct systematic document review (Bowen, 2009) to characterize source scope, content, accessibility, and credibility, generating detailed narrative descriptions of each source's features and limitations. Quantitatively, we score sources across seven quality dimensions (1–5 Likert scale), count corruption cases by type and sector, and tabulate convergence across sources (how many sources report the same case). This integration allows both rich contextual understanding and systematic comparison, supporting both narrative landscape description and evidence-based source ranking."

#### **4.2 Predominantly Qualitative**

While you use quantitative metrics, **your study is fundamentally qualitative** because:
- Your primary goal is **description and characterization**, not prediction or generalization
- You are **interpreting documents**, not testing hypotheses
- You emphasize **quality and context** over statistical significance
- Your analysis is **narrative and thematic**, not statistical

**Qualitative emphasis statement:**

"Although this study incorporates quantitative metrics (quality scores, frequency counts), it is fundamentally a qualitative research endeavor. The focus is on systematic description, critical appraisal, and contextual understanding of corruption data sources—their character, quality, accessibility, and interrelationships—rather than statistical hypothesis testing or predictive modeling."

## 7. Common Research Onion Questions & Your Answers

### **Q: Why not a fully quantitative approach?**

**A:** Your research question is fundamentally about *characterization* and *description*, not about quantifying relationships or testing hypotheses. While quantitative metrics (quality scores, case counts) are useful for systematic comparison, your primary goal—mapping the landscape—requires qualitative depth. A fully quantitative approach would collapse rich contextual understanding into numbers and miss the nuances of institutional biases, definitional variance, and accessibility barriers.

### **Q: Why abductive rather than deductive?**

**A:** Deductive research begins with a pre-specified hypothesis: "If X, then Y." Your study doesn't have a hypothesis to test; instead, you begin with a puzzle (fragmentation) and an observation (no unified data landscape). You propose that *systematic mapping* will reveal the landscape's structure, and this explanation is tested through empirical analysis. This is abductive reasoning: observing anomalies, proposing explanations, testing them.

### **Q: Why not purely exploratory?**

**A:** Purely exploratory research has no pre-existing framework; you would simply collect data and see what emerges. Your study is more structured: you have explicit inclusion criteria (PRISMA), a predefined quality framework (seven dimensions), and systematic procedures (phases 1–5). This structure makes it descriptive rather than exploratory. However, exploratory elements emerge when triangulation reveals unexpected patterns.

### **Q: How does critical realism justify mixed methods?**

**A:** Critical realism sits between positivism (one reality, objectively measurable) and constructivism (reality is socially constructed). It acknowledges that:
- **Corruption data are real** (objective) → justify quantitative counting, metrics, frequency analysis
- **But interpretation is mediated** (socially constructed) → justify qualitative assessment of credibility, context, bias

Mixed methods honor both ontological aspects: measure what is objectively measurable; interpret contextually where reality is socially mediated.

## 10. Critique & Reflexivity: Limitations of the Research Onion

The research onion is a useful framework, but researchers should be aware of limitations:

### **Criticisms**

1. **Linear presentation can mask iteration:** The onion suggests a top-down flow, but research is often iterative. Your philosophical position may be refined as you conduct research.

2. **Not universally applicable:** The onion was developed for business/management research. It may require adaptation for some disciplines (e.g., pure hard sciences may prioritize techniques over philosophy).

3. **False hierarchy:** The onion implies philosophy is "most important" and techniques are "least important." In practice, methodological techniques can reveal philosophical assumptions.

### **Your Reflexive Position**

In your proposal, you might acknowledge:

"While structured using Saunders' research onion framework, this research 
recognises that methodological choices are not entirely top-down. As data collection 
and analysis proceed, emergent insights may refine initial philosophical and 
design choices. We maintain reflexivity throughout, documenting any methodological 
adjustments and their rationale."
